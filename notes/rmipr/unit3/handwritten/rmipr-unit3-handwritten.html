1. Methods of Data Collection

Definition

Primary Data are gathered afresh and for the first time by the investigator, having an original character. Secondary Data are those already collected and compiled by an external agency, having passed through statistical processing.

1.1 Primary Data Collection Methods

  • Observation Method: Data gathered through direct monitoring without asking the respondent.
    • Structured vs. Unstructured: Structured defines specific observational units in advance; Unstructured monitors naturally without predefined categories.
    • Participant vs. Non-participant: In participant observation, the researcher actively joins the group being observed.
  • Interview Method: Oral verbal stimulus presented in face-to-face interaction or telephonically.
    • Structured: Uses predetermined, rigidly sequenced questions.
    • Unstructured: Flexible, conversational, exploring psychological nuances.
  • Questionnaires vs. Schedules:
    • A Questionnaire is mailed or handed to respondents to be read and answered by themselves without assistance.
    • A Schedule contains identical questions but is read aloud and filled out by a trained enumerator/investigator who visits the respondent in person.
Criterion Questionnaire Method Schedule Method
Administration Self-administered by the respondent. Filled out by trained enumerator in person.
Cost & Coverage Low cost, wide geographic reach. High cost, requires hiring and training enumerators.
Non-Response Rate High (many recipients discard forms). Very low (enumerator ensures completion).
Literacy Dependency Requires literate respondents. Applicable even for non-literate respondents.
Quick Recall

Primary data is gathered afresh (observation, interview, questionnaires, schedules). Questionnaires are self-filled by respondents; schedules are administered by trained enumerators.

2. Measurement & Scaling Techniques

Measurement is the process of mapping numbers or symbols to properties of objects according to specific rules. S.S. Stevens established four levels of measurement scales:

Scale Level Mathematical Properties Permissible Statistics Engineering Examples
1. Nominal Identity / Classification only (A=BA = BA=B or A≠BA \neq BA=B). No order or distance. Mode, Frequency, Percentage, Chi-square (χ2\chi^2χ2). Operating System type (Linux, Windows, macOS); Packet protocol (TCP, UDP, ICMP).
2. Ordinal Classification + Rank Ordering (A>B>CA > B > CA>B>C). Intervals between ranks are unequal/unknown. Median, Percentiles, Spearman's rank correlation. Software defect severity (Low, Medium, High, Critical); Likert agreement scales (1 to 5).
3. Interval Classification + Rank + Equal Intervals. Arbitrary (non-absolute) zero point. Addition & subtraction valid. Mean, Standard Deviation, Pearson's rrr, ttt-test, FFF-test. Temperature in Celsius/Fahrenheit; Calendar year (2025); Calendar time.
4. Ratio Classification + Rank + Equal Intervals + Absolute True Zero. Ratios (A=2BA = 2BA=2B) are physically meaningful. All statistics: Geometric mean, Harmonic mean, Coefficient of variation. Execution latency (microseconds), Memory usage (megabytes), Clock frequency (GHz), Weight.

2.1 Tests of Sound Measurement

  • Reliability: Consistency and repeatability of measurement. Assessed via Test-Retest reliability, Split-Half reliability, and Cronbach's Alpha (α≥0.70\alpha \ge 0.70α≥0.70).
  • Validity: Whether the instrument truly measures what it purports to measure.
    • Content Validity: Adequate coverage of all facets of the construct.
    • Criterion-Related Validity: Correlation with an external benchmark (Predictive or Concurrent).
    • Construct Validity: Alignment with underlying theoretical concepts (Convergent & Discriminant).
Quick Recall

Stevens' 4 scales: Nominal (labels) → Ordinal (ranks) → Interval (equal units, arbitrary zero) → Ratio (equal units, absolute true zero). Ratio permits multiplication and division.

3. Sampling Fundamentals & Types of Sample Design

Definition

A Sample is a finite subset of a population selected to estimate the parameters of that population. Sampling Design is the predetermined plan for obtaining a sample from a given population before any data are gathered.

3.1 Probability Sampling vs. Non-Probability Sampling

Category Technique Mechanism & Defining Feature
Probability Sampling
(Every element has a known, non-zero inclusion probability)		 Simple Random Sampling (SRS) Every item has an equal probability (1N\frac{1}{N}N1​) of selection via lottery or random number generators.
Systematic Sampling Select every kthk^{\text{th}}kth element from an ordered list after picking the first element randomly (k=N/nk = N/nk=N/n).
Stratified Random Sampling Population is partitioned into mutually exclusive, homogeneous strata (e.g. Freshmen, Sophomores, Seniors), and SRS is drawn from each stratum (Proportional or Disproportional).
Cluster Sampling Population is divided into heterogeneous naturally occurring clusters (e.g. server racks); a random sample of clusters is chosen, and all items in selected clusters are inspected.
Non-Probability Sampling
(Selection based on subjective human discretion)		 Convenience Sampling Units chosen based on immediate geographic proximity or ease of access.
Purposive / Judgment Sampling Researcher hand-picks units deemed most representative based on professional expertise.
Quota Sampling Interviewer fills specific demographic quotas (e.g. 50 female developers, 50 male developers) non-randomly.
Snowball Sampling Existing study subjects recruit future subjects from among their acquaintances (used for rare/hidden populations).
Quick Recall

Probability sampling (SRS, Systematic, Stratified, Cluster) eliminates selection bias and supports statistical inference. Non-probability (Convenience, Judgment, Quota, Snowball) relies on subjective judgment.

4. Foundations of Hypothesis Testing

Definition

A Statistical Hypothesis is an assertive, testable proposition concerning one or more unknown parameters of a population probability distribution.

4.1 Null Hypothesis (H0H_0H0​) and Alternative Hypothesis (H1H_1H1​)

  • Null Hypothesis (H0H_0H0​): The default hypothesis of "no difference", "no effect", or "no relationship" (e.g., H0:μ=320H_0: \mu = 320H0​:μ=320, or H0:p=0.20H_0: p = 0.20H0​:p=0.20). It is presumed innocent until proven guilty by compelling empirical evidence.
  • Alternative Hypothesis (H1H_1H1​ or HaH_aHa​): The assertion that a real effect, difference, or relationship exists (e.g., H1:μ≠320H_1: \mu \neq 320H1​:μ=320, H1:μ>320H_1: \mu > 320H1​:μ>320).

Every hypothesis testing exam question must strictly follow these five structured steps:

8. Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) & Covariance (ANOCOVA)

8.1 One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

While the ttt-test compares two means, ANOVA tests the equality of k≥3k \ge 3k≥3 population means simultaneously without inflating the Type I error rate:

H0:μ1=μ2=⋯=μkvs.H1:At least one mean differsH_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \dots = \mu_k \quad \text{vs.} \quad H_1: \text{At least one mean differs}H0​:μ1​=μ2​=⋯=μk​vs.H1​:At least one mean differs

ANOVA partitions total variance into two orthogonal components:

SSTotal=SSBetween+SSWithinSS_{\text{Total}} = SS_{\text{Between}} + SS_{\text{Within}}SSTotal​=SSBetween​+SSWithin​ F=MSBetweenMSWithin=SSBetween/(k−1)SSWithin/(N−k)F = \frac{MS_{\text{Between}}}{MS_{\text{Within}}} = \frac{SS_{\text{Between}} / (k - 1)}{SS_{\text{Within}} / (N - k)}F=MSWithin​MSBetween​​=SSWithin​/(N−k)SSBetween​/(k−1)​

8.2 Analysis of Covariance (ANOCOVA)

ANOCOVA combines ANOVA with linear regression. It removes the confounding effect of one or more uncontrolled continuous concomitant variables (covariates, XXX) that correlate with the dependent variable (YYY), thereby reducing residual experimental error and sharpening treatment comparisons.

Quick Recall

ANOVA compares k≥3k \ge 3k≥3 means using the FFF-ratio (MSBetween/MSWithinMS_{\text{Between}} / MS_{\text{Within}}MSBetween​/MSWithin​). ANOCOVA integrates regression to statistically adjust for confounding continuous covariates.

Comparison Dimension Observation Method Interview Method

Ramaiah Autonomous Examination • CIE-2 (Dec 22, 2025) Q3.b [7 Marks]

"Explain the steps involved in sample design."

Official Model Solution: Developing an authentic sample design follows six structured steps (C.R. Kothari Ch. 4):

  1. Type of Universe (Target Population): Define whether the universe of study is finite (e.g. 5,000 enrolled students) or infinite (e.g. continuous network packet streams).
  2. Sampling Unit: Decide the basic operational unit to be sampled before selecting items (geographical units like states/districts, institutional units like schools, or individual persons).
  3. Source List (Sampling Frame): Secure the authoritative register, roster, or directory containing every sampling unit from which the sample is drawn (e.g. university registrar enrollment database).
  4. Sample Size (nnn): Calculate the mathematically optimal number of items ensuring adequate precision, confidence level, and acceptable margin of error within budget constraints.
  5. Budgetary Constraints: Balance statistical variance reduction against real-world limitations of cost, compute time, and field investigator manpower.
  6. Sampling Procedure: Select the exact probability sampling method (Simple Random, Stratified, Systematic, Cluster, or Multi-stage) that minimizes sampling error.

PRACTICE 5 • TYPE I VS TYPE II ERROR TRADEOFF

Explain what happens to the probability of committing a Type II Error (β\betaβ) when an investigator reduces the significance level (α\alphaα) from 0.05 to 0.01.

📝 Practice Problem:
Show Step-by-Step Solution
Ans

For a fixed sample size nnn, α\alphaα and β\betaβ are inversely related. Reducing α\alphaα from 0.05 to 0.01 makes the critical boundary more stringent, decreasing the probability of a false alarm (Type I error). However, this expands the non-rejection region, increasing the probability of committing a Type II Error (β\betaβ) and lowering statistical test power (1−β1 - \beta1−β).
