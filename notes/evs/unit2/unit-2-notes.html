1. Natural Resources & Sustainability

Definition

A Natural Resource is any raw material, physical matter, or energy flow provided by the Earth that is essential or useful for sustaining human civilization and ecological integrity.

Classification Definition & Replenishment Rate Core Examples
Renewable Resources (Inexhaustible / Regenerative) Resources replenished naturally through biogeochemical or physical cycles at a rate equal to or faster than consumption. Solar radiation, wind kinetic energy, tidal flow, biomass (when harvested below maximum sustainable yield), freshwater.
Non-Renewable Resources (Exhaustible) Finite geological reserves formed over millions of years that cannot regenerate on human timescales once extracted. Fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas), metallic ores (iron, bauxite, copper), uranium, non-metallic minerals.
Quick Recall

Renewable = regenerated by natural cycles (solar, wind, water). Non-renewable = finite geological reserves (fossil fuels, metallic minerals).

2. Forest Resources & Deforestation

Forests are Earth's lungs, covering approximately $31\%$ of global land area. They serve critical economic functions (timber, pulp, resin, medicinal herbs) and invaluable ecological services (carbon sequestration, oxygen generation, hydrological regulation, soil stabilization).

2.1 Deforestation: Causes & Ecological Fallout

  • Principal Causes: Agricultural expansion (slash-and-burn / shifting cultivation), commercial timber logging, open-cast mining in forested belts, large-scale hydroelectric reservoirs, urban infrastructure corridors.
  • Consequences: Accelerated soil erosion, catastrophic loss of biodiversity, disruption of regional microclimate and rainfall cycles, increased atmospheric $CO_2$, and displacement of indigenous forest communities.

2.2 Dams & Forest Conservation Movements

  • Dams & Tribal Displacement: Mega-dam projects submerge thousands of hectares of prime virgin forest (e.g. Sardar Sarovar, Tehri Dam) and forcibly displace indigenous tribal populations without adequate rehabilitation.
  • The Chipko Movement (1973, Uttarakhand): Led by Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt; villagers (notably rural women) hugged trees to prevent commercial loggers from cutting them down.
  • The Appiko Movement (1983, Western Ghats, Karnataka): Inspired by Chipko, villagers in Uttara Kannada embraced forest trees with the slogan "Ubsu, Belasu, Balasu" (Save, Grow, and Wisely Use).
Quick Recall

Deforestation causes soil loss, climate shifts, and tribal displacement. Grassroots conservation: Chipko (Uttarakhand, Sunderlal Bahuguna) and Appiko (Karnataka Western Ghats).

3. Water Resources & Rainwater Harvesting

Freshwater constitutes only $2.5\%$ of global water reserves. Human extraction for agriculture ($70\%$), industry ($20\%$), and municipal use ($10\%$) is creating severe global water scarcity.

3.1 Over-utilization & Groundwater Depletion

  • Over-pumping: Deep tube-wells extract groundwater faster than monsoon recharge rates, plunging water tables downward by meters per year in agricultural breadbaskets (Punjab, Karnataka).
  • Land Subsidence & Contamination: Excessive pumping causes aquifer compaction, coastal saltwater intrusion, and mobilization of toxic geogenic fluoride and arsenic into drinking supplies.
  • Inter-State River Conflicts: Severe geopolitical disputes over shared river basins during dry spells (e.g. the Cauvery River Dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the Krishna Water Dispute).

3.2 Rainwater Harvesting (RWH)

Rainwater harvesting captures and stores rainwater for direct use or for recharging subterranean aquifers via recharge pits and borewells.

Formula: Annual Rooftop Rainwater Harvest Potential $$\mathbf{V = A \times R \times C}$$

where:

  • $V$: Total harvestable water volume ($m^3$).
  • $A$: Catchment roof area ($m^2$).
  • $R$: Annual rainfall ($m$ or $mm/1000$).
  • $C$: Runoff coefficient (fraction of rainfall not absorbed; $C \approx 0.80 - 0.90$ for concrete roofs, $0.60$ for tiled roofs).
Quick Recall

Over-extraction causes aquifer depletion and river disputes (Cauvery). Rooftop harvesting yield formula: $V = A \times R \times C$ ($m^3$).

4. Mineral Resources & Mining Impacts

Definition

A Mineral is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid substance possessing a definite chemical composition and an ordered internal crystalline atomic structure.

4.1 Environmental Fallout of Mining

  • Landscape Destruction: Open-cast surface mining strips overburden vegetation, creating massive craters and mine dumps.
  • Acid Mine Drainage (AMD): When sulfide minerals (e.g. pyrite, $FeS_2$) are exposed to air and water during mining, bacterial oxidation generates sulfuric acid ($H_2SO_4$), which leaches toxic heavy metals into streams: $$4FeS_2 + 15O_2 + 14H_2O \longrightarrow 4Fe(OH)_3 \downarrow + 8H_2SO_4$$
  • Air & Noise Pollution: Blasting, drilling, and hauling generate fugitive particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}, PM_{10}$) causing silicosis in miners.
  • Tailings Dam Failure: Collapse of wet tailing ponds releases millions of tons of toxic slimes into river networks.
Quick Recall

Minerals are naturally occurring inorganic solids. Mining causes land degradation, heavy metal toxicity, and Acid Mine Drainage (sulfide oxidation generating $H_2SO_4$).

5. Food Resources & Modern Agriculture

The Green Revolution dramatically increased grain yields through High-Yield Varieties (HYVs), synthetic fertilizers, chemical pesticides, and intensive canal irrigation, but triggered severe ecological side effects:

Modern Agricultural Practice Environmental Degradation Mechanism Ecological / Health Impact
Excessive Synthetic Fertilizers (Nitrates & Phosphates) Runoff into surface lakes causes Eutrophication (algal blooms, oxygen depletion, fish kills). Groundwater leaching causes Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia). Aquatic dead zones; impaired oxygen delivery in infants.
Persistent Organochlorine Pesticides (DDT) Lipid-soluble, non-biodegradable toxins accumulate up food chains via Biomagnification (concentration amplifies $\times 10^5$ from water to apex raptors). Eggshell thinning in birds, endocrine disruption, human carcinogenicity.
Intensive Canal Flood Irrigation Excessive water raises water table close to surface (Waterlogging). Evaporation concentrates dissolved mineral salts in topsoil (Salinization). Osmotic stress, plant root asphyxiation, cropland desertification.
Quick Recall

Agricultural side effects: Eutrophication & Blue Baby Syndrome (nitrates); Biomagnification (DDT concentration amplification); Waterlogging & Salinization (over-irrigation).

6. Land Resources & Soil Erosion

Soil is a non-renewable resource on human timescales, requiring centuries to form a single centimeter of topsoil through bedrock weathering.

6.1 Mechanisms & Types of Soil Erosion

A. Wind Erosion (Exam Focus: CIE 1 2024 Question):

  • 1. Saltation ($50-70\%$ of wind erosion): Fine-to-medium sand particles ($0.1 - 0.5\text{ mm}$) bounce along the surface in short hops, dislodging other particles upon impact.
  • 2. Surface Creep ($5-25\%$ of wind erosion): Larger particles ($0.5 - 2.0\text{ mm}$) too heavy to be lifted are rolled or pushed along the ground by colliding saltating grains.
  • 3. Suspension ($3-15\%$ of wind erosion): Extremely fine silt and dust particles ($< 0.1\text{ mm}$) are lifted high into the atmosphere and transported thousands of kilometers as dust storms.

B. Water Erosion:

  • Sheet Erosion: Uniform detachment and thin horizontal removal of topsoil by overland sheet runoff.
  • Rill Erosion: Concentrated runoff cuts small, shallow, distinct finger-like channels (rills).
  • Gully Erosion: Rills converge into deep, steep-sided channels (gullies) that dissect croplands into uncultivable ravines (badland topography).

6.2 Soil Conservation Measures

  • Contour Bunding & Terracing: Sculpting steep slopes into stepped terraces to slow surface runoff velocity.
  • Afforestation & Shelterbelts: Planting dense tree windbreaks perpendicular to prevailing winds to block wind erosion.
  • Mulching & Cover Cropping: Retaining crop residues on soil to prevent direct raindrop impact detachment.
Quick Recall

Wind erosion types: Saltation (bouncing grains, 50-70%), Surface creep (rolling coarse grains), Suspension (fine dust airborne). Water erosion: Sheet → Rill → Gully.

7. Energy Resources (Fossil Fuels vs. Renewable Alternatives)

7.1 Environmental Fallout of Fossil Fuels

  • Coal Combustion: Emits large amounts of $CO_2$ (greenhouse gas driving global warming), $SO_2$ and $NO_x$ (causing Acid Rain, $pH < 5.6$), particulate matter, and toxic fly ash containing heavy metals (mercury, lead).
  • Petroleum / Transportation: Major source of photochemical smog (ground-level ozone, $PAN$), carbon monoxide ($CO$), and marine oil spills.
  • Nuclear Power: Zero greenhouse gas emissions during operation, but carries catastrophic risk of core meltdown (Chernobyl 1986, Fukushima 2011) and unsolved challenges of long-term deep geological disposal of high-level radioactive waste (plutonium isotopes with half-lives exceeding $24,000$ years).

7.2 Renewable Energy Alternatives

  • Solar Energy: Photovoltaic (PV) cells convert sunlight directly into electricity via silicon semiconductor p-n junctions; solar thermal collectors generate heat/steam.
  • Wind Energy: Aerodynamic wind turbine blades capture atmospheric kinetic energy; clean, modular, zero operational water use.
  • Biomass & Biogas: Anaerobic digestion of animal manure and organic waste by methanogenic bacteria produces Biogas ($55-70\% \text{ CH}_4, 30-45\% \text{ CO}_2$), providing clean domestic cooking fuel while yielding nitrogen-rich organic slurry fertilizer.
  • Hydroelectric & Geothermal: Hydro harness gravitational potential of flowing water; Geothermal taps subterranean hydrothermal steam.
Quick Recall

Fossil fuels drive acid rain ($SO_2, NO_x$) and global warming ($CO_2$). Renewables (Solar PV, Wind, Biogas [methane]) provide decentralized, low-carbon power.

8. Solved Environmental Calculations

Solved Problem 2.1: Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting Yield

Problem Statement: A residential campus building has a flat concrete rooftop of dimensions $15\text{ m} \times 10\text{ m}$. The annual rainfall in the region is $900\text{ mm}$. If the runoff coefficient for the concrete roof is $C = 0.85$, calculate:

  1. The total volume of rainwater that can be harvested annually in cubic meters ($m^3$).
  2. The total volume in liters.

Step 1: Compute Catchment Roof Area ($A$):

$$A = 15\text{ m} \times 10\text{ m} = 150\text{ m}^2$$

Step 2: Convert Rainfall to Meters ($R$):

$$R = \frac{900\text{ mm}}{1000\text{ mm/m}} = 0.90\text{ m}$$

Step 3: Apply the Harvesting Formula ($V = A \times R \times C$):

$$V = 150\text{ m}^2 \times 0.90\text{ m} \times 0.85 = 135 \times 0.85 = \mathbf{114.75 \text{ m}^3}$$

Step 4: Convert to Liters ($1\text{ m}^3 = 1,000\text{ Liters}$):

$$V_{\text{litres}} = 114.75 \times 1,000 = \mathbf{114,750 \text{ Liters}}$$
$$\mathbf{V = 114.75 \text{ m}^3 = 114,750 \text{ Liters / year}}$$

Impact: This harvested volume can fulfill the 135 L/capita/day domestic drinking and sanitary needs of a 4-person family for over 212 days!

Solved Problem 2.2: Solar Rooftop PV Generation and Annual Carbon Offset

Problem Statement: An institution installs a $5.0\text{ kWp}$ rooftop Solar PV system. In Karnataka, each $1\text{ kWp}$ generates an average of $4.5\text{ kWh}$ of electricity per day. If the Indian coal-dominated power grid has an average emission factor of $0.82\text{ kg } CO_2/\text{kWh}$, calculate:

  1. The total electrical energy generated by the system annually.
  2. The annual carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) emission offset in metric tons.

Step 1: Annual Electricity Generation:

$$E_{\text{annual}} = 5.0\text{ kWp} \times 4.5\text{ kWh/kWp/day} \times 365\text{ days} = 22.5 \times 365 = \mathbf{8,212.5 \text{ kWh}}$$

Step 2: Annual Carbon Dioxide Offset:

$$\text{Offset} = 8,212.5\text{ kWh} \times 0.82\text{ kg } CO_2/\text{kWh} = \mathbf{6,734.25 \text{ kg } CO_2}$$

Step 3: Convert to Metric Tons ($1\text{ metric ton} = 1,000\text{ kg}$):

$$\text{Offset}_{\text{tons}} = \frac{6,734.25}{1,000} \approx \mathbf{6.734 \text{ metric tons of } CO_2}$$
$$\mathbf{E_{\text{annual}} = 8,212.5 \text{ kWh}, \quad \text{Annual } CO_2 \text{ Offset} = 6.734 \text{ Metric Tons}}$$

5-Mark Questions (From CIE 1 2024 Paper)

1. Global Warming & Climate Change: Massive $CO_2$ emissions trap infrared heat, accelerating glacier melting and extreme weather.

2. Acid Rain: Sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$) and nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) form sulfuric and nitric acids, acidifying soils and aquatic bodies ($pH < 5.6$).

3. Urban Smog & Air Pollution: Carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, and fine particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$) trigger severe respiratory illnesses.

4. Thermal & Land Degradation: Coal fly ash dumps contaminate groundwater; oil tanker spills devastate marine life.

1. Water Table Depletion: Pumping exceeds natural recharge, drying up shallow wells.

2. Saltwater Intrusion & Land Subsidence: Coastal over-pumping pulls seawater into freshwater aquifers; empty pore spaces collapse.

3. Waterlogging & Salinization: Over-irrigation in poorly drained soils drowns plant roots and deposits toxic surface salt crusts.

4. Inter-State Disputes: Conflicts between riparian states over river allocation during dry seasons (e.g. Cauvery dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu).
